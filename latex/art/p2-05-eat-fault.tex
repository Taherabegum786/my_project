\begin{formulas}
\begin{align*}
\text{EAT} &= (1-p)\times ma + p \times \text{page-fault service time}
\end{align*}
\end{formulas}
\noindent For example, with $ma = 200$ ns, a service time of 8 ms and $p = 0.001$:
\[
\text{EAT} \approx 200 + 0.001\times 8{,}000{,}000\text{ ns} \approx 8.2\ \mu\text{s}.
\]
A fault rate of only one in a thousand slows memory down forty-fold; this is why page-fault rates
must be kept tiny.
