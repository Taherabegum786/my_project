\section{Secondary Results}
\label{app:secondary}

\emph{Epoch controller (first-run costs).} An adaptive controller estimated the token rate (EWMA) and, at each epoch close, chose $\Delta$ from a 25-point logarithmic grid in $[0.01,60]$~s to minimize $D$ under size, signer-capacity and 2~s issuance-latency constraints. A demotion rule fixed in advance applied if it beat the best fixed $\Delta$ (chosen in hindsight) by less than 10~ms or 10\%. Over a 24-h diurnal workload (pull, per-tenant trees) it chose the 10~ms floor almost all day; its paired difference to the best fixed $\Delta$ was $-0.001$~ms [$-0.004$, 0.002] at $\bar\lambda=200$/s, $+0.005$~ms [0.001, 0.010] at 2000/s (10 seeds) and $-0.83$~ms [$-0.91$, $-0.75$] at \num{20000}/s (5 seeds, 1-h peak), so it was demoted. Work-conserving closing beat it by 4.93 and 4.41~ms at 200 and 2000/s, and fixed $\Delta\ge3$~s violated the SLO in every 5-min bin. Second-run costs moved $D$ by at most 0.1~ms (96.17$\to$96.08~ms). With stapling, the optimum is simply the smallest $\Delta$ the issuer can sustain.

\emph{Simulator consistency (first-run costs).} An independent event-driven simulator agrees with the Monte Carlo engine to within 0.56\% on misses per token, 0.09\% on $D$ and 0.08\% on issuance wait, over 45 metric rows ($\bar\lambda=200$/s, $E=100$, 1~h, 5 seeds). This checks the implementation for internal consistency only. It is not a validation.

\emph{Earlier latency campaign (P2, R1).} Superseded by the R2 results in Section~\ref{sec:res-edge}. At about 0.6 utilization, the added p50 relative to HMAC was $-2.60$~ms [$-6.29$, 0.55] for O1, +12.14~ms [3.17, 16.52] for B5 and +11.75~ms [2.61, 203.82] for B5v (per-run median, [min, max]); one B5v run collapsed, which we attribute, without verification, to the userspace link emulator carrying 3.3~KB URLs.

\emph{Earlier capacity campaign.} A quiet P1 campaign over 12 schemes, with edge utilization $\ge0.99$ in all 60 runs, measured edge CPU per request of 20.54\us{} for B1, 22.91 for O1, 22.93 for O4 (hybrid root), 37.38 for B5v, 66.24 for B5, 71.04 for Ed25519, 98.04 for ECDSA P-256 and 115.74 for the per-token hybrid.

\emph{Sensitivity.} An SLH-DSA-128s root puts its 306~ms signature on the issuance path and raises $D$ from 0.575 to 0.881~s (pull, $\Delta=1$~s). Halving the RTTs leaves staple-first $D$ at 0.503~s. The simulator's parameter defaults and sweep ranges are listed in Section~\ref{sec:method} and in the released \texttt{sim\_params.json}.
