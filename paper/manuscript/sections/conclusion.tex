\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
Post-quantum CDN tokens face two separate bottlenecks, and each has its own fix. At the edge, the problem is sending and verifying a 2.4~KB signature on every request. Stapling the signature once per edge connection solves it: in our prototype, per-token ML-DSA-44 then costs the edge as much as HMAC, with a 148-B URL token. At the issuer, the problem is one ML-DSA signature per token. Pooled epoch-Merkle batching (EMBER, a CDN profile of MTL-mode batch signing) solves it, cutting issuer CPU 19$\times$ at \num{2000} tokens/s and 144$\times$ at \num{20000} tokens/s. Batching pays only with pooled trees, and only above roughly $10^3$ tokens/s per issuer key. Per-tenant trees, which we previously recommended, save just 1.2$\times$. Header compression can hide large cookies, but only for clients that adopt a large HPACK table, such as Chrome. Open questions are measurements in a multi-PoP deployment, re-routes in the prototype, a simulator with queueing, and Firefox and Safari encoder behavior.
