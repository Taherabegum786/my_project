\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
Per-token post-quantum signatures do not fit CDN access tokens, and HMAC, although post-quantum, leaves a minting key on every edge. \ember{} applies a known primitive, Merkle batch signing, to this setting. It adds a token profile, dissemination of signed roots through the CDN, and stapling. With $2^{16}$ tokens per epoch, tokens are 503~B. Warm verification costs 2.34\us{} natively. A complete HTTP request costs 1.12$\times$ HMAC in edge CPU in our Go prototype, against 2.89$\times$ for per-token ML-DSA-44. An issuer core produces 1.28 million tokens/s. Stapling reduces the share of requests that wait for a root to 0.049\%. The measurements also bound the design. Verification is 7.9$\times$ HMAC rather than within 5$\times$. Adaptive epoch control gave no benefit over a 10~ms or work-conserving epoch. Period roll-up hardly reduces misses. With large HPACK tables, ML-DSA cookies lose most of their byte penalty. Delegated Ed25519 and KEM-wrapped MAC keys remain smaller alternatives for operators who accept their trust assumptions. Future work includes measurements in a real multi-PoP deployment, a simulator with edge queueing, and binding tokens to TLS exporters.
