\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
Post-quantum CDN tokens face two cost centers, the issuer and the edge, and they respond to different variables. At the issuer, the variable is the token rate per issuer key. Pooled epoch-Merkle batching (EMBER, a CDN profile of MTL-mode batch signing) cuts issuer CPU 19--144$\times$ in simulation and needs 0.38--1.08 cores where per-token signing needs 177 at the full token rates implied by Meta's CDN traces. It pays above roughly $10^3$ tokens/s per key and requires the pooled tenants to share an issuer key; in thin pools, trees hold one token and batching saves nothing. At the edge, the variable is the number of requests $K$ a token carries at one verifier. With session tokens ($K\approx150$), stapling the signature once per connection lets per-token ML-DSA-44 cost the edge as much as HMAC, with a 148-B URL token. With one token per object per hour ($K\approx2$ on the trace), the same design verifies a signature about every second request, and a pooled tree is 28$\times$ cheaper in edge crypto and 4.4$\times$ smaller on the wire. The crossover lies at $K\approx48$--154 for CPU and 9--27 for bytes. Real token granularity is the operator's choice and is not visible in public traces. [R2-PROTO-CONCL] Header compression can hide large cookies, but whether it does depends on each client. Delegated Ed25519 keys suffice only with a short $\vmax$. Open questions are traces with session or token identifiers, measurements in a multi-PoP deployment with re-routes, a simulator with queueing, and Firefox and Safari encoder behavior.
