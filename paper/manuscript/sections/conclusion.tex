\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
Post-quantum CDN tokens face two separate bottlenecks, and each has its own fix. At the edge, the problem is sending and verifying a 2.4~KB signature (a 3.3~KB encoded token) on every request. Stapling the signature on the first request of each connection, with a retry on request, solves it: in our prototype, per-token ML-DSA-44 then costs the edge as much as HMAC, with a 148-B URL token. At the issuer, the problem is one ML-DSA signature per token. Pooled epoch-Merkle batching (EMBER, a CDN profile of MTL-mode batch signing) solves it, cutting issuer CPU 19$\times$ at \num{2000} tokens/s and 144$\times$ at \num{20000} tokens/s. Batching pays only with pooled trees, which require tenants to share an issuer key, and only above roughly $10^3$ tokens/s per key with OpenSSL's signer, or $3.4\times10^3$/s with circl's. Per-tenant trees, which we previously recommended, save just 1.2$\times$. Header compression can hide large cookies, but whether it does depends on each client's table limit, indexing policy and other headers. Delegated Ed25519 keys suffice only with a short $\vmax$, because published estimates reach about 10~minutes per key. Open questions are measurements in a multi-PoP deployment, prototype runs of the recommended configurations with re-routes, a simulator with queueing, and Firefox and Safari encoder behavior.
