# Budget evidence for papers/benchmark/experiments/profiles.yaml

Re-read from full text (all six fetched in full): twardokus2024when (NDSS PDF, 2024-267-paper.pdf), junquera2023assessment (arXiv 2312.11080), darzi2026a (arXiv 2606.30542), goertzen2022post (arXiv 2211.14196), wiethuechter2024drip (RFC 9575 txt), abrar2026deployment (arXiv 2608.05087).
"Derived" = my arithmetic from stated numbers, not a source statement.

## cv2x (twardokus2024when)

(a) "the maximum payload size (considering only practical data rates) in a standard 10 MHz channel is a mere 437 bytes [44, Table A.8.3-1]" (Sec. IV, C-V2X vs DSRC paragraph). Also: "under IEEE 1609.2, one SPDU is at most 226 bytes using implicit vs. 330 bytes using explicit certificates" (Sec. II background). Other sizes: "|SPDU| = 24 + |BSM| + |certificate| + |sigBSM|" and "|F| = 40+|SPDU|" (about 40 B encoded-structure overhead per frame, Sec. V); ECDSA certificate 162 B; Table III frames (DSRC): Falcon F1 = 1026 B, later frames 204 B; certificate-only learning response 172 B; DSRC cap 2,304 B.
(b) 437 B covers the whole C-V2X payload (BSM + SPDU headers + certificate/digest + signature). The 226/330 B figures cover a whole classical ECDSA SPDU (already containing signature and certificate). Neither is an auth-only field. The paper does not say whether 437 includes the 40 B frame overhead.
(c) Auth bytes: not stated. Derived only with outside numbers; the paper gives no BSM size. Conclusion the paper does draw: 437 B "is insufficient to contain even a PQ signature" (Falcon-512 signature is 666 B in other sources).
(d) BSM every 100 ms; certificate cycle 500 ms; added delay 0.39 ms mean (light/dense averages 0.25 and 0.39 ms stated). Loss: median frame loss ratio rises by 0.11 (+28.9%) light and 0.26 (+61.9%) dense for Partially Hybrid alone (Table VI, DSRC/VEINS, not C-V2X). No C-V2X deadline stated.
(e) profiles.yaml: WRONG as an authentication budget. 437 is a whole-payload ceiling (the yaml note admits this but still uses it as budget_bytes and frame_payload). Also the profile uses 10 MHz; frag_hdr is not 0 (40 B frame + 24 B SPDU overhead stated). Corrected auth budget: not stated by source; mark TODO-RESULT, or label as "payload ceiling 437 B" and subtract overheads explicitly.

## osnma (junquera2023assessment)

(a) "DSM Block n 104" bits; header 8 (NMA) + 4 (DSM ID) + 4 (BID), total 120 bits per HKROOT message (Table 1). "all the information must be allocated in 16 DSM Blocks at most" (Sec. 2, HKROOT). Sec. 3 (Assessment conclusion): "each one is 104 bits long, we have 1664 bits available for these two use cases". Use case 1: "Discarding the 16 bits of metadata, there are available 1632 bits for cryptographic material... The shortest Falcon public keys take 7176 bits, so they would need 71 DSM Blocks". Use case 2: "discarding the 104 bits of metadata, the maximum space available for the DS field... would be 1727 bits, 79 bits per DSM Block. As the shortest Falcon signature is 5328 bits long, there would be necessary 67 DSM Blocks".
(b) 1664 bits = a whole DSM (all 16 blocks) of either type, including its fixed fields. 1632 = DSM-PKR minus 16 bits metadata, shared with a 1024-bit Merkle signature and public key. The signature-only field space is smaller (see c).
(c) Per-block accounting is NOT consistent in the source. 16 x 104 = 1664 is fine. But 1664 - 104 = 1560, not the quoted 1727; 16 x 79 = 1264, not 1727; 79 x 67 = 5293 < 5328, so ceil(5328/79) = 68, not 67 (derived). 608 free bits / 16 = 38, but source says 32 bits per block. 7176/(1632/16 = 102) = 70.35, giving 71 only if the Merkle signature is ignored (derived). The 67/71 block counts are what Falcon would NEED, not available space. Defensible auth budget from the source: 1632 bits = 204 B for a DSM-PKR cryptographic material, and about 1264 bits = 158 B for the DS field in DSM-KROOT (derived from 79 bits x 16). 208 B (1664 bits) includes header/metadata. Table 6 gives max lDK = 1456 bits, lDP = 1664 bits.
(d) One HKROOT message (one DSM block) per subframe; "A Sub-frame is transmitted every 30 seconds"; full OSNMA frame 720 s (24 subframes). Proposed BID extension to 7 bits would make the message take "142s instead of 30s". No loss figures.
(e) profiles.yaml: PARTLY WRONG. frame_payload 13 B (104-bit block) correct. budget 208 B = 1664 bit is the whole 16-block DSM including metadata, not auth data; corrected: 204 B (1632 bit, PKR case) or about 158 B (DS field, KROOT case, from the 79 bits/block figure), flagged as derived/inconsistent. The 67/71 reproduction: source figures cannot be reproduced exactly (68 by the source's own 79 bits/block).

## sib1 (darzi2026a)

(a) "a maximum size of 372 bytes per [SIB1 transport block]" (Sec. II background); "SIB1 is 372 B every 20-160 ms". "srsRAN gNB utilizes 79 bytes out of the allowed 372 bytes... with 293 bytes available per packet for authentication material" (Sec. VI). Table III: ML-DSA 2-level certificate 9,884 B crypto, 12,648 B OTA, 34 packets; FN-DSA 2-level 3,792 B crypto, 4,836 B OTA, 13 packets; EMULSION |C|=2: 324 B crypto, 744 B OTA (2 packets), E2E 160.07 ms.
(b) 372 B is the whole SIB1 transport block (SIB content plus auth). The 34/13 fragment counts are for full two-level certificate chains (signature plus certificate), not a bare signature. 324 B is cryptographic overhead for the whole |C|=2 chain: first packet 351 - 79 = 272 B plus 52 B (derived) = 324 B, spread over 2 packets, not per packet. Epoch-opening packet carries about 351 B; steady-state packets 131 B with 52 B auth overhead; ">= 241 B headroom" steady-state.
(c) Auth bytes per packet: 293 B (stated: 372 - 79 B SIB1) for that testbed message.
(d) SIB1 period 160 ms (configurable 20-160 ms). 10% loss: "Auth. Success @10% Loss" EMULSION 90.0%, FN-DSA 25.4%, ML-DSA 2-level 2.8%; retransmission overhead about 41 B vs about 1,971 B (FN-DSA). Delay = (packets - 1) x 160 ms plus processing. No hard deadline stated except bootstrapping before RACH.
(e) profiles.yaml: PARTLY WRONG. 372 is correct as the block ceiling but budget_bytes should be 293 B (stated, for a 79 B SIB1). frag_hdr stays not stated.

## dns (goertzen2022post)

(a) "the recommended maximum DNS response size, including any signatures and public keys, should not exceed 1232 bytes"; "accounting for the size of IPv6 headers, it is recommended that the DNS message sizes should not exceed 1232 bytes" (Sec. 1/2). Fragmentation cost: RRFRAG layout (Fig. 1): NAME 1 B, TYPE 2, CLASS(RRID) 2, TTL(CURIDX) 4, RDLENGTH 2, FRAGSIZE 2, RRSIZE 2 = 15 B (derived), and the text states "RRFRAGs in requests are 15 bytes in size". "the most common ARRF request in our experiments was 60 bytes including UDP, IP, and DNS message headers, and the largest request being 75 bytes"; TCP ACK 52 B. Measured bytes with 1232 limit: Falcon-512 2,557 vs 3,112 standard.
(b) 1232 B covers the entire DNS message (header, question, all RRs, signatures, keys). Not a signature-only field.
(c) Auth bytes: not stated. The paper does not give DNS header/question/other-RR sizes needed to derive it.
(d) Lossless only ("Our experiments only considered the case of lossless packet delivery"); BIND9 default timeout 800 ms; 10 ms latency scenario resolution 61.96-82.11 ms for Falcon-512. Resolver requirement from cited work: validate at least 1000 signatures/s, sign 100 records/s.
(e) profiles.yaml: budget 1232 is UNSUPPORTED as an auth budget (message ceiling only). frag_hdr can be filled: 15 B per RRFRAG in requests (responses use the same RR layout; 11 B RR header plus 4 B in RDATA by derivation). Corrected auth budget: not stated.

## remote_id (wiethuechter2024drip, RFC 9575)

(a) "Bluetooth 4.x ... can only transmit 25 octets of payload per frame" (Sec. 3.2.4.1); page: "Page Header: (1 octet)", "Authentication Payload: (23 octets per page)", "Authentication Headers: (6 octets)", "Authentication Data / Signature: (0 to 255 octets)" (Sec. 3.2.1-3.2.2). Binding rule (Sec. 3.2.4.2): "Authentication Data / Signature data MUST fit in the first 9 pages" and "The Length field ... MUST NOT exceed the value of 201." SAM Authentication Data 0 to 200 octets. Appendix B: "(201 - 17) / 23 == 8" (page 0 holds 17 B of data after the 6 B headers).
(b) 255 is the ASTM F3411 field maximum; RFC 9575 caps it at 201 B (includes SAM Type, excludes Additional Data) because Bluetooth 5.x extended transport holds 9 pages and DRIP requires all transports to fit. The 23 B per page covers headers plus data (page 0 only 17 B of data); the 2 B/page "header" (25 - 23) is 1 B page header plus 1 B not itemized in RFC text read (message type/version octet per F3411, not verified).
(c) Auth bytes: 201 B total, SAM data 200 B (stated). With FEC on Bluetooth 4.x: FEC goes in Additional Data, up to 16 pages, 361 B ceiling.
(d) Rate: static Authentication Messages "at least every 3 seconds", dynamic (DRIP Wrapper) "at least every 1 second"; Message Pack 1 per second. Loss: Bluetooth 4.x has no FEC; DRIP single-page XOR FEC corrects one lost page only. No numeric loss figure or deadline.
(e) profiles.yaml: WRONG on budget_bytes (255 should be 201); frame_payload 23 and frag_hdr treatment acceptable but page 0 carries only 17 B data. Corrected auth budget: 201 B. (A Falcon-512 666 B signature exceeds this by arithmetic; the RFC specifies no PQ scheme.)

## abrar2026deployment (conflict with our result)

What it claims: Falcon-512 is "the only post-quantum candidate that fit the applicable transport-block constraints"; Dilithium-2 and SPHINCS+ exceed capacity. Assumption: SAE J3161 profile, 20 MHz channel, 100 RB, 10-RB subchannels, 10 subchannels; low-speed MCS set {5,6,7,11}; "MCS 11 with a maximum allocation of 10 subchannels yields a TBS ceiling of 2,481 bytes" (19,848 bits). Message sizes: SPDU = 28 B header + 39 B BSM core + signature + certificate (Eq. 1). Table 2: ECDSA 64 B sig, 170 B cert, 301 B SPDU; Falcon-512 666 B sig, 1,002 B cert, 1,735 B SPDU (Table 3 prints 1,739); Dilithium-2 3,904 B. Digest mode (8 B HashedId8): Falcon 741 B SPDU, 4 subchannels at MCS 11; certificate mode 7 subchannels at MCS 11 (70% of resources), N/A at MCS 5/7. Cert sent every 5th BSM (J2945/1); the larger certificate-bearing SPDU is RLC-fragmented over two periods when the grant is sized for a digest, raising loss. BSMs 10 Hz (100 ms RRI). Results: mean latency about 52 ms, 95th percentile 97-98 ms, 90% PDR only at LOS level of service A (ECDSA through C); neither meets 90% in NLOS. Limitation stated: simulated certificate transmission differs from a fully compliant implementation.
Explanation of the conflict: Twardokus uses 437 B (10 MHz, "practical data rates", 3GPP table) as the maximum payload, so a 666 B Falcon signature alone does not fit; Abrar uses the J3161 20 MHz profile with 10 subchannels at MCS 11 (2,481 B), so a 741-1,735 B Falcon SPDU fits in one TB. "Feasible" in Abrar means fits a transport block, not that the PDR requirement holds (90% only at LOS level of service A). Abrar does not cite the 437 B figure (only Twardokus's other papers).

## One-line verdicts

- cv2x: wrong as auth budget (437 B is whole payload); corrected auth budget not stated (TODO-RESULT); frag/frame overhead 40 B + 24 B SPDU stated.
- osnma: partly wrong; 208 B is whole 16-block DSM; use 204 B (1632 bit, PKR) or about 158 B (79 bit x 16, KROOT DS, derived); 67/71 not reproducible (68 by source's own 79 bits/block).
- sib1: partly wrong; 372 B ceiling correct, auth budget 293 B (for a 79 B SIB1).
- dns: unsupported as auth budget; 1232 B is whole message; frag header 15 B per RRFRAG request stated.
- remote_id: wrong; 255 should be 201 B (RFC 9575 Sec. 3.2.4.2).
- abrar2026deployment: no profile; 2,481 B TBS assumption (J3161, 20 MHz, MCS 11, 10 subchannels) vs 437 B explains the conflict.
